# Logarithmic Laplacian: semilinear equations, Lipschitz boundaries and nonexistence in star-shaped domains

Vadim Chekmachev · chekmachev@phystech.edu · ORCID [0009-0009-2982-0532](https://orcid.org/0009-0009-2982-0532)

DOI of all versions: [![10.5281/zenodo.24199751](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.24199751.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24199751)

## Abstract

The logarithmic Laplacian L<sub>Δ</sub>, the operator with symbol 2 ln |k|, is the derivative of the fractional Laplacian (−Δ)<sup>s</sup> with respect to s at s = 0. In domains of class C<sup>1,1</sup> the solution of the Dirichlet problem for it decays near the boundary no slower than (ln(1/d))<sup>−1/2</sup>, where d is the distance to the boundary. There the factor of this power, the boundary coefficient, is continuous and replaces the normal derivative in the Pohozaev identity. For semilinear equations with a smooth nonlinearity, bounded solutions lie in W<sup>1,1</sup> and satisfy the Pohozaev identity. For the logarithmic nonlinearity f<sub>0</sub>(x, u) + λu ln |u| with λ ≥ 0 and a function f<sub>0</sub>, Lipschitz in x, whose modulus of continuity in u is o(r ln(1/r)), the Pohozaev identity is proved without this regularity. Hence, under a strict Pohozaev-type condition, in particular for ωu + λu ln |u| with constant ω and λ &gt; 4/N, the nonexistence of nonzero bounded solutions in star-shaped domains of class C<sup>1,1</sup> follows. For a constant ω and λ = 4/N, nonzero nonnegative solutions are thus excluded. For λ ≤ 4/N and linear growth of f<sub>0</sub> every weak solution is bounded, whereas for λ &gt; 4/N boundedness may fail. The continuity of the boundary coefficient persists on boundaries of class C<sup>1</sup> with a Dini-type normal and may fail on general C<sup>1</sup>-boundaries. In Lipschitz domains the boundary coefficient exists almost everywhere on the boundary, a criterion for its existence is given, and an example of a point where it does not exist is provided, while the Pohozaev identity for linear equations remains valid. At a cone vertex, the decay exponent is determined by the fraction of the sphere occupied by the cone. No a priori assumptions on the smoothness of the solution are made.
